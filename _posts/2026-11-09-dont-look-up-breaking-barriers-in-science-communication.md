Have you watched the movie *Don’t Look Up* yet?

Apart from its glaring message on climate change, one key takeaway point for me as a student of environment engineering and an avid consumer of science and climate-related literature was the urgent need for scientists and technologists to abandon the jargon and data overload, and instead, break down complex information into digestible bits.

Throughout the movie, Dr. Randall Mindy (played by Leonardo DiCaprio), and Dr. Kate Dibiasky (played by Jennifer Lawrence) tend to engage in complex science jargon. As they grapple with the imminent threat of a comet hurtling towards Earth, their discussions are laden with technical terminology and intricate scientific concepts, all the time struggling to convey the urgency of the situation.

The film serves as a stark reminder of the importance of breaking down complex information into digestible terms to engage and mobilize the public in the face of existential threats.

Science, the bedrock of societal progress and sustainability, often struggles to find a receptive audience. Why? Because it’s not easily understood by most of us.

There’s a tendency among academics to think that letting the data or facts speak for themselves is enough. But it is not. The random data-spewing only confuses people further, prompting them to switch off. And worst, leaving them to cling to their biases and prejudices.

Whether it’s climate change or debates over vaccination amid the pandemic, in this era of information overload and dwindling attention spans, effective communication from scientists is more critical than ever. Rather than inundating the public with complex data and scientific jargon, they must distill information into easily understandable nuggets. Analogies, visuals, and real-world examples can help bridge the gap between scientific expertise and public comprehension.

Furthermore, scientists and experts must be willing to engage in open, transparent dialogue with the public. This means actively addressing concerns, debunking myths, and fostering trust through clear, honest communication. It’s not enough to simply present the facts; scientists must connect with their audience on a human level, acknowledging their fears and uncertainties.

At the same time, policymakers must recognize the importance of evidence-based decision-making and prioritize scientific expertise in policy formulation. By fostering collaboration between scientists, policymakers, and the public, we can navigate complex issues like climate change and public health crises more effectively.

Effective communication is the linchpin of scientific progress and public understanding. As we confront pressing global challenges, from climate change to pandemics, scientists and communicators must embrace a new paradigm of engagement. By breaking down barriers, fostering dialogue, and prioritizing clarity, we can ensure that science remains accessible and relevant to all.
